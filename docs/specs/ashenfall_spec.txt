5. Population Instructions: How to Force Still Life to Generate Where Desired
Still Life features (branched canopy trees, fallen birch/oak logs, glacial erratic boulders, and wildflower carpets) do not rely on
random brush strokes. They are governed by three deterministic mechanisms that can be programmatically forced:
Method 1: The WorldPainter 'Populate' Layer (100% Guaranteed Native Decorator Pass):
When pre-generating terrain via WorldPainter or Python, never export chunks as Status: 'full'. Instead, apply the binary
ASHFALL_POPULATE_MASK.png to WorldPainter's native Populate layer. When Minecraft loads a chunk flagged with
Populate, its internal world decorator natively executes Still Life's entire placed feature registry. Valleys receive lush trees and
fallen logs; cliffs and volcanic calderas remain completely bare.
Method 2: Still Life Biome Tag Injections (Datapack Level):
Still Life feature placement is bound to biome tags in data/still_life/tags/worldgen/biome/. By adding target biomes into these
tags, Still Life is forced to generate in those specific biomes:
# data/still_life/tags/worldgen/biome/has_canopy.json -> ["minecraft:plains", "minecraft:forest"]
# data/still_life/tags/worldgen/biome/has_fallen_logs.json -> ["minecraft:forest", "minecraft:taiga"]
# data/still_life/tags/worldgen/biome/has_boulders.json -> ["minecraft:windswept_hills", "minecraft:meadow"]
Method 3: The Still Life Slope-Aware Surface Rule:
Still Life inspects local geological slope angles. Tree and log decorators require grass/dirt surface blocks. Terrain steeper than
$35^\circ$ automatically strips topsoil to bare rock scree, naturally preventing trees from generating on cliff faces:
